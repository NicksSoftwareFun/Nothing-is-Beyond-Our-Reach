# Pineland Burning {#ch-pineland-burning}

::: flavor
Nailed to the door of the Corbin's Mill feed store, October of the fourth year, over the peeling census schedule: *"BY ORDER OF THE DISTRICT ADMINISTRATOR — SEASONAL CONSOLIDATION OF FUEL, MEDICAL STORES, AND ROLLING STOCK FOR THE WINTER CONTINUITY PERIOD. PERSONS WITH KNOWLEDGE OF UNREGISTERED STORES ARE REMINDED OF THEIR DUTY TO REPORT."* It hung one full day. By the second morning somebody had penciled along the bottom margin, small and level: *they are counting gallons again. so are we.* The clerk who took the notice down for filing carried that pencil line into the annex in her head, whole. Nobody ever taught her to do that.
:::

This is LEAF SEASON: six linked scenarios, October into November, one valley. Run each night with the mission frame of {{ref:ch-running-missions}}; run the weeks between with {{ref:ch-long-war}}. This chapter adds what those chapters cannot: the ground, the enemy's actual dispositions, and the connective tissue — every scenario ends in a CONSEQUENCES block whose lines rewrite the later sheets. Play the six in order; what order cannot survive, {{ref:sec-campaign-flow}} handles.

## The Valley in Leaf Season {#sec-leaf-season}

The Sorrel comes down out of the Blue Ridge through a granite gorge, goes flat and red-brown past Corbin's Mill, and runs east until it stops being mountain water and starts being somebody else's river. Route 9 keeps it company the whole way. Everything the valley is hangs off that pairing like gear off a belt: Prewitt Dam plugging the gorge in the northwest, its wires marching east to an enclave nobody from here has seen the inside of; the town — a feed store, a dead mill, a courthouse annex with new padlocks and old clerks; the crossroads at Dutton's Cross where the upland farms come down to the blacktop; and the hollows, stacked north of the river like knuckles on a fist — Sugar Creek, Bray Hollow, a dozen more — each with one road in, one creek out, and a memory that predates every map the district owns.

The Emergency Continuity Authority administers all of it from paper. District Nine runs on the census ledger, the requisition schedule, and the radio log: a checkpoint on the Route 9 bridge, a Civil Order Corps depot in the old co-op, patrols that keep to the blacktop, and a repeater on Gault Ridge that stitches the whole apparatus to the enclave. The professionals are few and mostly elsewhere. The Corps is local, numerous, and knows whose barn is whose. Four Octobers in, the arrangement has stopped pretending to be temporary: the works farm takes the census evaders, the fairground takes the confiscated trucks, and the winter consolidation order takes everything else.

October is the valley's one soft month. Hunting season puts rifles in truck racks legally; wood smoke covers cook fires; the canopy still holds, and a figure who knows the ground can cross the district in a day and never be a name in the log. It will not last. The leaves are coming down scene by scene, the sightlines opening with them, and the first hard frost is already in the long-range weather. The crew — twelve neighbors, one machine gun, one graph-ruled composition book — stands where every resistance stands at the start: trusted a little, unproven, short of everything but reasons. The G-chief's people watch from the high hollows and wait to see whether these twelve are worth burying beside.

Six nights will answer that.

## The Six Nights {#sec-campaign-flow}

The spine is fixed; the state it carries is not. Between scenarios, run the full between-mission sequence ({{ref:sec-between-missions}}) — quiet turns, events, and Hard Choices land where they land, and the weather rows in each briefing assume roughly a week between nights.

**Reading the maps.** Every board is the standard 30×24 grid, printed with edge coordinates: columns lettered **A to Z, then AA to AD**, west to east; rows numbered **1 to 24** from the north edge down. A reference like N12 means column N, row 12. North is up on every map. Scripted posts, patrol routes, and named ground are printed on the map face — the Warden hides nothing but what the Blips hide ({{ref:sec-warden-blips}}).

**Carrying consequences.** At each aftermath, after the standard scoring ({{ref:sec-aftermath}}), read the scenario's CONSEQUENCES block and write every line that came true directly onto the later sheet it names. Six scripted nights against a procedural enemy could have gone two bad ways: a railroad, or a reset. Nothing carried forward is a reward or a punishment; it is the district, updating its paperwork. ## Scenario 1 — Eyes on the Valley {#sec-s1}

*"First rule of the glass: see everything, be nothing." — Deacon's OP card, taped inside the mission book*

**BRIEFING.** WHAT: the district resupply run — enclave to the Corbin's Mill depot, Route 9, Thursdays. Watch it pass. Count it. WHY: winter is a number of gallons, and theirs is written on the side of a truck. WHO: six, quiet feet, one long gun. ALSO: the G-chief is sending a man to the old sawmill on Bray Creek. Meet him, take his hand, walk him out. He is the beginning of everything. IN: over Gault Ridge, last dark. OUT: the same ridge, before the change of watch. Nobody knows we were ever off it.

**THE MAP.** Standard 30×24 board — the Sorrel bend below Gault Ridge, where Route 9 and the river run together at the mouth of Bray Hollow.

The Sorrel crosses the south third (rows 16–19, impassable); Route 9 follows its north bank from AD14 to A16, bridging Bray Creek at N15 — the creek runs down column N from the hollow mouth at N1 and joins the river at N18, with a crawlable culvert under the road at N15. A powerline cut runs down columns U–V, rows 1–14. Full-canopy hardwood covers the slopes; conifer stands at G4–J7 and R3–T6 block deep LOS.

**FORCES.** Crew: up to 6; one Scoped weapon recommended. No support units — the contact becomes one. The Warden's force is Civil Order Corps ({{ref:sec-faction-coc}}):

- **Post 9** — scripted sentry pair (2× Corpsman, Holding, radius 6): the bridge checkpoint at O15/P15, one facing west over the bridge, one facing east up the road. Landline in the shack at O14 — their ALARM is automatic at the post ({{ref:sec-alarm}}).

- Alerted or worse → standard aftermath riders only; the district's log now has a shape in it that looks like you.
- Eli Bray dies on the board → Rapport −2 over the standing riders. The G-chief sent one man to see what you were. He saw.

- ALARM reached → S3's off-shift fireteam starts Posted, not Deep. The relay heard the district stand to.
- The driver is a volunteer, not a Corpsman. If he dies in the crossfire, the valley counts it: Rapport −1 over the standing riders.

## After the Last Night {#sec-epilogue}

Score Scenario 6 like any mission, run one last between-mission sequence for the wounded and the lost, then read the log front to back — tracks, roster, memorial page last, out loud if anyone is with you ({{ref:sec-campaign-end}}). The campaign does not grade you. It asks you to say, honestly, which season you just played:

- **The valley holds.** The sweep turned back and at least 8 came out the gap — or never had to leave. The Authority's winter map has a blank space with your river on it. If Rapport ends 8+, the ending is the one the motto promised: the hollows feed their own, the G-chief's cells stand their own watches, and the crew's next war is teaching. If Heat ends 8+ anyway, the blank space is a target — build the spring with {{ref:sec-architect-campaign}}.
- **The long walk.** The column made it out and the hollow did not: the crew bought the evacuation with the valley itself. Count the cost — the roster, the till, the names — and know this too is a win state: everyone the Authority never got is a line in a ledger they cannot balance. The campaign continues in the north country if you want it: the winter camp, the ice road, the lake kits of {{ref:sec-architect-terrain}}.
- **The ash reading.** Fewer than 8 out, or the crew broke before the column cleared. Read the memorial page, then read Rapport: at 2+, the valley still hides what is left of you, and spring starts from a cellar instead of a church — harder, colder, not over. At 0–1 it is over, whatever the roster says ({{ref:sec-campaign-end}}); write the final page and name where everyone lies.

Overlay the other two tracks on whichever season you got. **Capability** is the future tense: at 6+, the valley now holds people who can do this without you — say so in the ending. **Heat** is the past tense: the bill for every loud win, and it transfers to whoever stays.

Then, if the table wants more — tables usually do — the war has been leaving hooks all season: the lockup with a name in it, the works farm's census evaders, the Ferrymen's unpaid favor, the passes if TRIBUTE closed them, every asset lost with a location attached. Build the winter from them with {{ref:ch-mission-architect}} at your final tracks. LEAF SEASON ends when the passes close. Nothing says the reach does.

::: designer
The finale's two win states are the design's whole thesis. A campaign about neighbors cannot end at "kill the sweep" — sometimes the sweep is simply bigger than the valley, and the honest victory is ten people over a gap in the snow, bought at retail. So S6 is built as a judgment scene, not a boss fight: the special win exists and is genuinely reachable off a well-run campaign (low drone coverage, a delayed ALARM, a paid-off Compact, a deep bench), but the mission's floor — the column, the counting of heads — is a thing you can hold even while losing the fight. Falling Skies taught the stakes; Robin Sage graded the relationship. The last night grades both, and the memorial page is the transcript.
:::
